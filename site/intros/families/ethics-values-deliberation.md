label: Ethics & Values Deliberation
tagline: Take a moral or values trade-off as the input and reason to a defensible position across everyone it affects, instead of optimizing inside a frame that has already quietly decided whose good counts.
---

These frameworks work on the values dimension of a decision: not which option scores highest on your own criteria, but whose wellbeing, autonomy, and fairness are at stake, and what a position that holds up across all the affected parties actually looks like. The shared move is to refuse the frame's default answer to "good for whom" and make the moral structure explicit, by removing your own position from the choice, by gridding stakeholders against principles, or by naming the harms a success would create. Reach here when a decision carries a real moral trade-off and the honest answer is contested, not when a single clear criterion already settles it. The evidence in this family is thinner than the decision families, and each skill says so on its face: these structure a deliberation, they do not adjudicate it.
