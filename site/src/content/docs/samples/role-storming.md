> A compact worked example of [Role-Storming](../../frameworks/think-role-storming/) on a realistic situation. For full end-to-end narratives see the [Showcase](../../showcase/); for the evidence behind the method see its [framework page](../../frameworks/think-role-storming/).

## Situation

Mira's 8-person company ships a scheduling tool, and new-signup activation is flat: most people create an account, poke around once, and never come back. Every time the team brainstorms fixes, the conversation collapses onto the same idea within five minutes - "we need a better tutorial video" - and a couple of the louder engineers steer everything back to it. Mira knows the product cold; the block is fixation plus a little embarrassment about floating "unserious" ideas in front of the team. She wants a wide, weird pool of activation candidates before anyone argues about which to build.

## Prompt

```
/think-role-storming "Our signup-to-activation is dead - people sign up, look once, and ghost. Every brainstorm just turns into 'make a better tutorial video' and we never get past it. Give me a wide pile of ideas to get a brand-new user to their first real win, before we pick anything."
```

## The artifact

# Persona-Tagged Idea List

> This is a DIVERGENT pool. Do NOT rank, score, or down-select inside this artifact; that is a separate convergence step. Every persona is uninhibited or deliberately foreign - an inhibited persona (a cautious auditor, a risk-averse executive, a rigid bureaucrat) suppresses ideas instead of freeing them.

---

## Generative question

- **Question:** What could we do to get a brand-new signup to their first real "this is useful" win, fast - beyond making a better tutorial video?
- **Why role-storming fits:** Mira's team is not short on product knowledge; it is fixated on one frame ("activation = teach them the UI better") and the brainstorm keeps snapping back to the tutorial-video idea, with some self-consciousness about floating odd ideas. The block is fixation plus inhibition, not missing facts. This pool will feed a convergence step (a `think-decision-option-review` on the strongest 6-8 candidates, scored against build effort and the activation thesis).

## Personas (each uninhibited or deliberately foreign)

| Persona | Why this persona frees or shifts thinking |
|---|---|
| A frazzled new parent who has 90 seconds before the baby wakes | Strips away "learn the tool" framing; forces ideas that deliver value instantly, with zero patience for a tour |
| A theme-park ride designer obsessed with the first 30 seconds | Pulls associations toward sensation, momentum, and an engineered "first thrill" instead of feature explanation |
| A street magician who has to hook a stranger before they walk off | Breaks the inhibition about being showy; generates surprise-and-reveal ideas the "serious B2B" room would not voice |
| A concierge at a five-star hotel who never says "figure it out yourself" | Shifts the frame from self-serve onboarding to "we do the first thing FOR you," reaching done-for-you candidates the tutorial frame misses |
| A speedrunner who optimizes any task to its absolute minimum steps | Deliberately foreign standpoint; surfaces ruthless shortcut and skip-the-setup ideas the careful-onboarding frame would not reach |

(Persona gate at work: a tempting "cautious customer-success lead" persona was deliberately NOT used. It is inhibited and narrowing and would have generated caveats and risks, not ideas. Those concerns belong downstream in the evaluation, not in this divergent pool.)

## Persona-tagged ideas

### As the frazzled new parent

- Skip account setup entirely on the first run - let me schedule one real thing in the first 60 seconds, then ask me to sign up to keep it. - *(new parent)*
- Pre-fill everything you can guess from my calendar invite or email signature so I am editing, not building from a blank page. - *(new parent)*
- Let me do the whole first task by replying to one email or text, so I never even open the app to get a win. - *(new parent)*

### As the theme-park ride designer

- Open with a "watch this" moment: drop the user into a pre-loaded sample week that visibly resolves a messy double-booking in front of them, before they touch anything. - *(ride designer)*
- Give immediate, satisfying motion - every action snaps something into place with a visible payoff, so the first 30 seconds feel like progress, not a form. - *(ride designer)*
- Engineer a single guaranteed "first win" everyone hits, then end the ride cleanly with one obvious next thing to do. - *(ride designer)*

### As the street magician

- A "paste your messy availability, watch it become a clean bookable link" reveal - the value is the surprise transformation, done live in one step. - *(magician)*
- Show the user something they did not know was possible in their first session (a self-resolving conflict, an auto-suggested best slot) so they lean in instead of leaving. - *(magician)*
- Hand them a shareable artifact at the end of the first run (a polished booking page with their name on it) - the reward is the thing they can show off. - *(magician)*

### As the five-star concierge

- For the first task, do it FOR them: "tell us the meeting you need and we will set it up," then show the finished result rather than teaching the steps. - *(concierge)*
- Offer a 10-minute white-glove setup call to new signups who look stuck, and let them book it in one tap from inside the dead-end screen. - *(concierge)*
- Greet each new user by the specific job they signed up to do (captured at signup) and walk them straight to that, skipping every feature they did not come for. - *(concierge)*

### As the speedrunner

- Cut the setup to its irreducible minimum: detect the one input you actually need, ask only for that, and defer the rest forever. - *(speedrunner)*
- Offer a "fastest path" mode that does the absolute shortest route to a booked event and hides every optional setting until later. - *(speedrunner)*
- Let power-curious users import an existing setup (a calendar, a competitor's link) in one move so they reach a working state instantly instead of configuring from zero. - *(speedrunner)*

## Caricature sweep

One magician "idea" was dropped because it had degraded into pure performance (a flashy animation with no underlying value to the user) - that is the persona mugging, not generating. The concierge voice was watched so it stayed about real done-for-you product mechanics, not a generic "delight the customer" platitude. Nothing here imports a crude stereotype of a real group.

## Hand-off to convergence

- **Status:** divergent pool - NOT ranked, scored, or down-selected here.
- **Most surprising / promising to look at first (optional, not a ranking):** the "first real action before signup" (new parent) and "do the first task FOR them" (concierge) both reframe activation away from "teach the UI"; the "paste messy availability, watch it transform" reveal (magician + ride designer converging) is the most concrete first-win candidate; the email/text-only first task (new parent) is the most ambitious and the most uncertain.
- **Next step:** take the strongest 6-8 candidates into a `think-decision-option-review` to evaluate them against build effort, fit, and the activation thesis - that convergence and judgment is explicitly NOT this artifact's job.

---

*Evidence caveat: role-storming is a long-lived, named ideation heuristic with a plausible mechanism (an assumed identity lowers ownership inhibition and shifts your default associations), but there is no controlled study measuring role-storming itself against ordinary brainstorming, so the "more ideas" framing is a practitioner claim, not a measured effect. The one hard adjacent result sets the persona gate: an uninhibited persona helps and an inhibited one HURTS, which is why every persona above is freeing or deliberately foreign and none is a cautious narrowing role. The value here is breadth: five non-self standpoints surfaced pre-signup activation and done-for-you moves that the standing "better tutorial video" brainstorm had fixated away from.*

## Why this framework fits

Role-storming's job here is to break the fixation that kept every brainstorm collapsing onto "make a better tutorial video," by changing WHO is generating rather than asking the same people to try harder. The artifact gives Mira a wide, persona-tagged pool of activation candidates - including pre-signup and done-for-you angles the default frame never reached - and hands it cleanly to a convergence step instead of prematurely picking one safe idea.
