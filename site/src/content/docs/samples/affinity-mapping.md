> A compact worked example of [Affinity Mapping](../../frameworks/think-affinity-mapping/) on a realistic situation. For full end-to-end narratives see the [Showcase](../../showcase/); for the evidence behind the method see its [framework page](../../frameworks/think-affinity-mapping/).

## Situation

Priya runs the public-comment intake for a regional transit agency's proposed fare restructure, which would replace flat fares with distance-based pricing. The 30-day window closed with 140 written comments - a mix of online-form submissions, emailed letters, and transcribed voicemails. Her director wants a "what are people actually saying" memo by Friday, and Priya does not want to impose the agency's three pre-drafted talking points on the pile before she has read what is in it.

## Prompt

```
/think-affinity-mapping "I have 140 public comments on our distance-based fare proposal - online forms, emails, transcribed voicemails. I need to synthesize them into the real themes before I write the response memo. Don't sort them into our existing talking points; let the themes come from what people actually wrote, and keep each theme traceable back to comments."
```

## The artifact

> **Affinity Map (Clustered Theme Map)**

## Synthesis subject

- **Question:** What are commenters actually saying about the distance-based fare proposal, so the response memo answers the real concerns rather than our pre-drafted talking points?
- **Source of items:** 140 public comments from the 30-day window (92 online-form submissions + 31 emailed letters + 17 transcribed voicemails).
- **Item count:** 140 discrete comments.

## Themes and what they tell us (summary)

The pile collapses into six themes. Two dominate and pull in opposite directions: **equity harm to outer-zone riders** (people in farther, lower-income neighborhoods would pay the most under distance pricing) and **fairness of paying for what you use** (shorter-trip and inner-city riders welcome no longer subsidizing long commutes). The memo cannot treat the proposal as simply popular or unpopular - the split is structural, by where people live. A mid-sized theme, **fare-capping and transfers must be protected**, is the most actionable signal: many commenters do not oppose distance pricing outright but condition support on a daily cap and free transfers. The two smallest clusters, technology and reliability worries, are real but adjacent to the fare question itself. The headline: support and opposition track geography, and a daily cap plus transfer protection is the lever most likely to convert conditional opponents.

## Outliers / parking lot

- "Make it all free and fund it from parking revenue" - one off-question proposal to abolish fares entirely; parked, not a fare-structure comment.
- "The online comment form was broken on mobile" - process complaint about intake, not the proposal; one-off, flagged separately to the web team.
- "Add a night-owl route to the east side" - a single service-request comment; could be an early signal if more route-specific asks accumulate in a future window, but a singleton here.

---

*Evidence caveat: affinity mapping is a practitioner standard (the KJ method; Kawakita 1967) with a plausible basis in external representation and chunking, but it has no strong controlled evidence that it yields more accurate or less biased themes than another synthesis method, and "group by felt similarity" stays a subjective judgment - see the [framework page](../../frameworks/think-affinity-mapping/). The value here is that the six themes emerged bottom-up from 140 comments rather than from the agency's three pre-drafted talking points, each theme traces back to verbatim signal, and the geographic split plus the thin technology and service clusters are flagged for what they are rather than laundered into a confident summary.*

## Why this framework fits

Affinity mapping's job here is deferred, bottom-up categorization: by clustering 140 comments before naming anything, it lets the real fault line (geography, not just pro/con) surface instead of forcing the pile into the agency's existing talking points. The artifact gives Priya a weighted, traceable theme map - including the actionable "protect the cap and transfers" lever - that a confident summary paragraph would have flattened and detached from its evidence.
