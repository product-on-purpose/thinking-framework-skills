> A compact worked example of [Scenario Planning (2x2)](../../frameworks/think-scenario-planning/) on a realistic situation. For full end-to-end narratives see the [Showcase](../../showcase/); for the evidence behind the method see its [framework page](../../frameworks/think-scenario-planning/).

## Situation

Priya, a policy analyst at a mid-size city's transportation agency, has been asked to recommend whether the city should commit its 10-year capital budget to a fixed-rail light-rail line down its busiest corridor, versus a flexible bus-rapid-transit (BRT) program that can be re-routed. Rail locks in the route and the cost for a generation, BRT keeps options open but signals less permanence to developers. The decisive forces - how people will commute after the remote-work shift settles, and whether state and federal transit dollars keep flowing - are years out and entirely outside the agency's control. She runs the framework by hand before drafting the council memo.

## Prompt

```
/think-scenario-planning "I'm advising city council on a 10-year transit capital
bet: fixed light rail down the main corridor vs. flexible bus rapid transit. The
two things I can't predict are where commuter demand settles after remote work and
whether state/federal transit funding holds up. I keep catching myself assuming a
'return to 2019 ridership plus generous federal grants' future. Build me the
scenarios so I can stress-test both options instead of riding one forecast."
```

## The artifact

> The worlds are structured speculation, not probabilities. Do NOT rank them by likelihood or act on "the most likely quadrant." The payoff is the robustness read and the signal watch-list, not the stories.

### Focal decision and horizon

- **Focal decision:** Commit the 10-year capital budget to a fixed light-rail line down the primary corridor, versus a re-routable bus-rapid-transit program.
- **Horizon:** 10-15 years - long enough that commuting patterns and the intergovernmental funding climate, not construction logistics, decide whether the bet pays off.
- **Uncontrollable domain:** how the region travels and how transit is paid for - post-remote-work commuter demand and the flow of state and federal capital dollars. The agency controls the route, the mode, and the service plan; it controls neither of these.

### Driving forces and predetermined elements

- **Predetermined elements (high impact, low uncertainty):** continued population growth in the metro; an aging bus fleet that must be replaced this decade regardless; rising construction-cost inflation; a durable climate mandate to cut vehicle emissions. These shape every world and are not in contention.
- **Critical uncertainties (high impact, high uncertainty):** (1) where corridor commuter demand settles - **concentrated** (people return to dense, peak-hour, downtown-bound travel) or **dispersed** (hybrid and remote work permanently flatten the peak and scatter trips across the day and the map); (2) the intergovernmental funding climate - **flush** (sustained state and federal capital grants) or **lean** (grants dry up, the city carries the cost alone). A third candidate, local political will, was set aside as not independent of the funding climate: lean funding erodes the will to build.

### The two axes

- **Axis A: Corridor demand** - spectrum from **concentrated** (dense peak-hour downtown commuting returns) to **dispersed** (hybrid work flattens the peak and scatters trips).
- **Axis B: Funding climate** - spectrum from **flush** (sustained state and federal capital grants) to **lean** (grants dry up, the city pays alone).

(These two are genuinely independent: whether commuters re-concentrate is a behavior question, whether grant money flows is a fiscal-political one, and neither determines the other.)

### The 2x2 - four named worlds

|  | **Axis B = flush funding** | **Axis B = lean funding** |
|---|---|---|
| **Axis A = concentrated demand** | **World 1: Rail's Moment** | **World 2: Crowded and Broke** |
| **Axis A = dispersed demand** | **World 3: Gold-Plated Ghost Line** | **World 4: Do More With Less** |

- **World 1: Rail's Moment** (concentrated x flush) - The textbook case for rail. Peak-hour downtown ridership returns in force and the grants are there to build. A high-capacity fixed line down a corridor that is genuinely full pays for itself in throughput and anchors transit-oriented development. Rail is in its element; the only fights are construction timeline and station siting.

- **World 2: Crowded and Broke** (concentrated x lean) - Demand justifies rail, but the money to build it has evaporated. The city faces packed buses it cannot afford to upgrade and a corridor crying out for capacity it cannot finance. A committed rail bet here becomes a half-funded mega-project that stalls mid-build; phased, pay-as-you-go BRT is the only mode the budget can actually deliver.

- **World 3: Gold-Plated Ghost Line** (dispersed x flush) - The dangerous world, because the money tempts a mistake. Grants flow, so rail looks affordable, but hybrid work has permanently scattered demand and the dense peak never returns. A fixed line gets built down a corridor that no longer concentrates riders - a beautiful, expensive train running half-empty, impossible to re-route toward where demand actually went. Flexibility, not capacity, is what this world rewards.

- **World 4: Do More With Less** (dispersed x lean) - The squeeze. Demand is scattered AND the money is tight. Neither a fixed line nor an expansive build is viable. Success means a lean, frequently re-routed bus network chasing dispersed demand at the lowest possible capital cost, plus partnerships (micro-transit, demand-responsive service) to cover the long tail.

### Robustness read

How the two options and candidate moves fare across the four worlds.

| Move | World 1 Rail's Moment | World 2 Crowded and Broke | World 3 Ghost Line | World 4 Do More With Less | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commit the budget to fixed light rail | wins | fails (stalls unfunded) | fails (half-empty, unmovable) | fails | **bet** - wins only in World 1 |
| Build phased, re-routable BRT | survives (lower ceiling) | wins | wins | wins | **robust** - works in 3 of 4, never strands the budget |
| Reserve the corridor right-of-way for future rail | helps | helps | low cost, low harm | low cost, low harm | **robust** - keeps the rail option alive cheaply |
| Design BRT to a "rail-convertible" standard (stations, dedicated lanes) | wins | partial | wasted if never converted | wasted if never converted | **bet on the concentrated column** |
| Stand up demand-responsive micro-transit partnerships | low value | helps | helps | wins | **bet that pays in the dispersed column** |

- **Robust moves (survive every world):** **build phased, re-routable BRT** as the committed spine (it delivers in three worlds and never strands the capital budget mid-build), and **reserve the corridor right-of-way for future rail** (cheap insurance that keeps World 1's upside reachable). Commit to both regardless of which future arrives.
- **Bets (win in one world, lose in another):** committing the budget to fixed rail is a bet on **World 1** (concentrated + flush); going heavy on micro-transit partnerships is a bet on the **dispersed column** (Worlds 3-4). Make each consciously, not by sliding into the "2019-plus-grants" default.
- **Gaps (no current move covers):** **World 2** (concentrated + lean) is the exposure - demand the city must serve and no money to serve it well. No move above fully covers it; it argues for a pre-negotiated financing contingency (a standby bond authorization, a regional cost-share) funded as an option now.

### Signal watch-list (which world is arriving)

| World | Leading indicators that this world is arriving |
|---|---|
| World 1: Rail's Moment | Peak-hour corridor boardings recover toward pre-2020 levels; downtown office occupancy climbs; a multi-year federal capital grant is awarded |
| World 2: Crowded and Broke | Boardings recover but the federal program is cut or the state rescinds match; bus crowding complaints rise while the capital plan goes unfunded |
| World 3: Gold-Plated Ghost Line | Grants stay generous but the AM peak never reforms; trip origins keep scattering off-corridor and into midday; ridership-per-mile keeps falling |
| World 4: Do More With Less | Both grants thin out and demand stays dispersed; ridership flattens across the day and the map; micro-transit pilots outperform fixed routes on cost per rider |

### Options to keep open

Because the worlds diverge, three options are worth holding even though committing to any one now would be premature: (1) the **reserved rail right-of-way**, kept clear so a fixed line can still be built fast if World 1 signals fire; (2) a **rail-convertible BRT design standard** (dedicated lanes, station footprints) staged so the spine can be upgraded if demand re-concentrates; and (3) a **standby financing instrument** (pre-authorized bond capacity, a regional cost-share agreement) held against World 2's funded-demand gap. Keeping these open is the payoff of having modeled four futures instead of building for the one that felt most familiar.

*The scenario set is a divergence-and-robustness aid, not a forecast. Scenario planning is a long-established practitioner method with a coherent rationale and only thin controlled evidence (honest read M-down-to-P), and none studies an AI-produced set; treat the four worlds as structured speculation that surfaces robust moves and signals, never as ranked probabilities or a measured improvement in outcomes. See its [framework page](../../frameworks/think-scenario-planning/) for the full evidence read.*

## Why this framework fits

Priya was quietly optimizing for one future - "2019 ridership plus generous grants" - and scenario planning forces that bet into the open by crossing the two forces she cannot predict into four contrasting worlds. The artifact gives her what an unaided pro-and-con memo would not: a robust spine (phased BRT plus a reserved right-of-way) that holds whichever future arrives, the named gap (funded demand with no money) to design a contingency against, and a watch-list that tells the council which world is actually showing up.
