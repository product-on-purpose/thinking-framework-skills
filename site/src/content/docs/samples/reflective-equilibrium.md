:::caution[Reflective equilibrium is a contested lens]
Reflective equilibrium is tier C: philosophically central since Rawls (1971) but empirically untested as a procedure. "Equilibrium" has no external stopping test, the inputs can be a reshuffling of prejudices (Brandt 1979), and the method can license rationalizing a preferred outcome; even idealized, convergence is rare (Freivogel 2023, ~27 percent). This sample runs it honestly: the caveat leads, all three tiers are shown, and a revision ledger records what gave way. For an impartial rule under uncertainty prefer [veil of ignorance](../../frameworks/think-veil-of-ignorance-reasoning/); this fires only because reflective equilibrium was asked for by name, explicit-request-only.
:::

## Situation

Maya chairs the grants committee of a small science foundation. The committee's written rule is "fund only proposals with preliminary data." A proposal from an early-career researcher has no preliminary data but a strong design and a problem nobody else is touching, and Maya's gut says rejecting it would be a mistake. She wants the conflict between the rule and the case adjudicated honestly, not just talked into a tidy answer.

## Prompt

```text
/think-reflective-equilibrium "Our grants rule says 'fund only proposals with preliminary data,' but I strongly feel we should fund this no-data, high-design early-career proposal. Reconcile the rule with the case and show me what gets revised."
```

## The artifact

> **Evidence caveat (read first):** Reflective equilibrium is tier C; philosophically central since Rawls (1971) but untested as a procedure, with no external stopping test, a risk of reshuffling prejudices (Brandt 1979), and a license to rationalize. Convergence is rare even idealized (Freivogel 2023, ~27 percent). What follows is worth reading only because the ledger shows which commitment gave way and why.

### The question

- Should the "preliminary-data-required" rule apply to this proposal, or is the judgment that funding it is right load-bearing enough to revise the rule?

### Tier a - Considered judgments (particular cases)

- Rejecting this strong-design, no-data proposal would be a mistake `[held strongly]`
- A rule managers waive whenever they "feel" strongly invites bias and is unfair to rejected applicants `[held strongly]`
- Funding a vague proposal just because it lacks data and feels novel would be irresponsible `[held weakly]`

### Tier b - General principles

- Require preliminary data so funded work is de-risked `[held weakly]`
- Fund the most promising science the evidence of merit supports `[held strongly]`
- Apply the same published criteria to every applicant `[held strongly]`

### Tier c - Background theories (the wide variant)

- Theory of research merit: preliminary data is one signal of feasibility, not feasibility itself; design quality is an independent signal the rule ignores.
- Procedural fairness: published, consistent criteria protect applicants from arbitrary discretion, which is why the "no waivers" worry is real.

### Conflicts found

- "Rejecting this would be a mistake" collides with "require preliminary data."
- "Ad hoc waivers are unfair" collides with simply overriding the rule for this one case.

### Revision ledger

| Conflict | Commitment that gave way | Direction | Why it was the weaker member | Alternative considered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong case vs. "require preliminary data" | The data-required principle | case revises principle | It was held weakly and treats a proxy (data) as the goal; the merit theory shows design quality is an independent feasibility signal the rule misses | Overruling the case and rejecting, declined because the merit judgment was strong and the rule's basis was thin |
| "Waivers are unfair" vs. "just override it here" | The case judgment (its broad waiver form) | principle overrules case | The fairness worry holds; a *published* successor criterion (data OR a design-quality threshold) keeps criteria consistent without case-by-case discretion | One-off exception for this applicant, declined because it re-imports the arbitrariness |

### Residual disagreements (stop honestly)

- Where to set the design-quality threshold, and whether the foundation's board will accept the revised criterion, is unresolved. I reconciled this case; I did not ratify a new funding policy. Coherence here is local, not global.

## Why this framework fits

Maya asked to reconcile a rule with a conflicting case, so a flat refusal would not help. Run caveat-first, the lens earns its keep: laying out all three tiers, running the revision in both directions, and recording the ledger surfaces that the data rule was a weak proxy and produces a fairer successor criterion instead of a quiet override. Unaided, "reconcile these" tends to narrate to a tidy "it all coheres" with no record of what was traded away, which is the rationalization failure mode the caveat warns about. If Maya next needs a funding rule that is fair across all future applicants, [veil of ignorance](../../frameworks/think-veil-of-ignorance-reasoning/) is the stronger move.
