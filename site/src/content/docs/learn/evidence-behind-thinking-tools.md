For the skeptic who assumes "thinking tools" means recycled consultant decks and laundered statistics. The bet of this library is the opposite: every empirical claim traces to a graded source, the strong-evidence core is named, and where the evidence is thinner than people assume, the page says so. This path is for tracing the grading, not for running a method. Bring suspicion; follow each link to its grounding.

## The path

1. **Start at the grading model.** Read [The evidence model](../../start/evidence-model/) for the seven tiers (S/M/P/V/A/C/X) and the rule that a "P, useful anyway, here is when not to use it" beats a dressed-up "S". The tier grades the framework's mechanism, not a vendor's confidence.
2. **Go to the aggregated bibliography.** The [bibliography](../../evidence/bibliography/) collects the graded sources across the library so any claim can be walked back to its primary source. No effect size appears unless a source records it.
3. **Inspect the strong-evidence core.** The S-tier skills are the anchor: replicated experimental or meta-analytic support, not practitioner lore. Each one carries its own dossier with sources and limits: [Brainwriting](../../frameworks/think-brainwriting/), [Far-Analogy Ideation](../../frameworks/think-far-analogy-ideation/), [Stocks and Flows Reasoning](../../frameworks/think-stocks-and-flows-reasoning/), [Authentic Dissent](../../frameworks/think-authentic-dissent/), [Argument Mapping](../../frameworks/think-argument-mapping/), [Natural-Frequency Bayesian Framing](../../frameworks/think-natural-frequency-bayesian/), [Linear-Model Aggregation](../../frameworks/think-linear-model-aggregation/), [Reference Class Forecasting](../../frameworks/think-reference-class-forecasting/), [WOOP (Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions)](../../frameworks/think-woop/), and [After Action Review](../../frameworks/think-after-action-review/).
4. **Read the honest part.** A strong tier does not mean an unlimited claim. The caveats below come straight from those dossiers.

## The honest part

The interesting thing about an S grade is what it does *not* cover. These limits are pulled from the dossiers, not invented.

- **[Argument Mapping](../../frameworks/think-argument-mapping/):** the large measured gains in critical-thinking skill are for *sustained practice*, typically a course-length program building the skill. A single one-shot map does not carry the course-length effect, and a tidy map shows valid structure, not true premises.
- **[Authentic Dissent](../../frameworks/think-authentic-dissent/):** genuine minority dissent improves a group's reasoning, but role-played or assigned devil's advocacy does *not* replicate it. An AI cannot supply the authentic dissent the evidence is about; the skill engineers the conditions for real dissent rather than pretending to be the dissenter.
- **[WOOP](../../frameworks/think-woop/):** the strong evidence is for closing the intention-action gap, and it is conditional. Positive fantasizing about the outcome *alone*, skipping the obstacle and the if-then plan, measurably *reduces* follow-through. Skip the hard steps and you invert the effect.
- **[Stocks and Flows Reasoning](../../frameworks/think-stocks-and-flows-reasoning/):** the strong result is that a specific accumulation error is real and widespread, and that making the stock-flow structure explicit removes it on a given problem. It is not a claim that the skill teaches general systems thinking, where the pedagogy evidence is mixed.

A related caveat lives outside the S-tier and is worth naming because it is the most common overclaim in this whole field: the [premortem](../../frameworks/think-premortem/) reliably surfaces more and more-specific risks, which is well supported, but it is *not* proven to improve final outcomes. The library grades the mechanism that is demonstrated and refuses the one that is not.
