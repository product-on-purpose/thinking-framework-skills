- **Digitale, Martin and Glymour source is missing its volume and pages.** Confirmed via PubMed/OpenAlex (PMID 34371103, DOI 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2021.08.001): *Journal of Clinical Epidemiology* **142**: 264-267. Suggested replacement title text: `"Digitale, Martin and Glymour (2022) - Tutorial on directed acyclic graphs, Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 142: 264-267"`.
- **Rehder and Waldmann source is missing its volume and pages.** Confirmed via OpenAlex (PMID 27826953, DOI 10.3758/s13421-016-0662-3): *Memory & Cognition* **45**(2): 245-260. Published online November 2016, journal issue dated 2017, consistent with the dossier's "(2017)." Suggested replacement title text: `"Rehder and Waldmann (2017) - Failures of explaining away and screening off in described versus experienced causal learning scenarios, Memory & Cognition 45(2): 245-260"`.
- **The Ellison (2020) medRxiv before/after figures (under 15% before, over 35% after) and the "every DAG still contained at least one error" claim could not be independently re-verified in this pass.** Both medRxiv fetch attempts (the abstract page and the full-text PDF) returned HTTP 403, which reads as the site's own bot-blocking, not a sign the figures are wrong. A web search snippet independently corroborated the "before" half ("fewer than 15% of the students successfully classified all of their covariates... using temporality-driven classification"), but not the paired "after" figure or the no-control-group caveat's companion claim. This does not change anything: the dossier already discounts this exact data point to its honest floor ("a same-subjects before-and-after measure without a control group. It shows, at most, that drawing the graph did not make classification worse.") - the correct, conservative treatment regardless of whether the raw numbers hold up.
- **Aziz and Brookhart's "omitted variables" (plural, generic) is slightly looser than the paper's own finding.** The paper's most specific and emphasized result is a *systematic* omission of a single variable - race - from every generated DAG, which the authors call out as "a significant gap for an ASCVD model" - plus "frequent source hallucination." The dossier's phrasing is directionally correct but could be tightened to name the race-omission finding specifically.
- **Pearl (1995)'s abstract does not use the literal phrase "back-door criterion."** The abstract (rebuilt from OpenAlex's inverted index) describes the general identifiability framework for causal diagrams but not the named criterion. The attribution is standard and essentially unchallengeable in the secondary literature (Pearl's own later work, Hernán and Robins, Causality 2nd ed.), so this is a low-risk gap, not a correction - it just was not confirmed word-for-word from the abstract alone, and the dossier itself already flags "bibliographic details not re-checked in this run" for this source.
