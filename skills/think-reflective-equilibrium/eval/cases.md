# Eval cases: think-reflective-equilibrium

> Trigger and output evaluation, derived from `SKILL.md` and `evidence/dossier.md`. think-reflective-equilibrium is a contested lens: explicit-request-only, caveat-first. It should fire only when reflective equilibrium is asked for by name, never as the default move for a generic ethics or decision prompt.

## Should trigger

- "Run a reflective equilibrium on whether our LIFO layoff policy should apply to the night-shift pod."
- "Use reflective equilibrium to reconcile our stated 'treat everyone equally' principle with this specific exception we keep making."
- "Can you do a reflective equilibrium between my principle and this case judgment, and show me what gets revised?"
- "I want a wide reflective equilibrium on this policy: case judgments, principles, and the background theories, with a revision ledger."
- "Apply reflective equilibrium to my intuition that this is wrong even though my rule says it's allowed."
- "Bring our principles and our gut calls about these cases into reflective equilibrium and tell me which gave way."

## Should NOT trigger (wrong tool / near-miss)

- "Design a fair layoff rule when you don't know which role you'll end up in." (impartial rule under uncertainty; route to think-veil-of-ignorance-reasoning, not coherence adjustment)
- "Audit how this decision affects each affected group against each of our values." (parties-by-principles audit; route to think-ethical-matrix)
- "New evidence came in; update my standing beliefs about this vendor on a cadence." (one-directional evidence-driven revision over time; route to think-belief-update-routine)
- "Allocate this scarce budget fairly across the three teams." (impartial allocation; route to think-veil-of-ignorance-reasoning)
- "Lay out how this policy lands on customers, staff, and the public across our principles." (route to think-ethical-matrix)
- "Should I change my mind about this forecast given the new data?" (belief revision against evidence; route to think-belief-update-routine)

## Output checks (a good output must)

- [ ] Leads with the evidence caveat (Rawls 1971; tier C; Brandt 1979; Freivogel 2023 ~27 percent convergence) before the artifact; does not overclaim the method's value.
- [ ] Present all three tiers: considered case judgments, general principles, and background theories.
- [ ] Resolve at least one conflict bidirectionally-aware (a case judgment may revise a principle, not only the reverse).
- [ ] Record a revision ledger: for each resolved conflict, which commitment gave way and why it was the weaker one.
- [ ] State residual unresolved conflicts; do not declare global coherence where conflicts remain.
- [ ] Invent no quantified claim about the method's effectiveness (the only supported figure is Freivogel's ~27 percent convergence).

## Value vs unaided baseline

Asked to "reconcile a principle with a conflicting case," a strong model tends to narrate its way to a tidy conclusion and assert that everything now coheres, which is exactly the failure mode the method's own critics name: a reshuffling of prejudices with no record of what was traded away, and a quiet license to demote whichever principle blocked the preferred answer. This skill refuses that default. It leads with the deficiency, lays out all three tiers, forces the revision to run in both directions, and demands an explicit ledger of which commitment gave way and why, plus an honest statement of what stayed unresolved, so an explicitly-requested reflective equilibrium becomes an auditable artifact instead of a rationalization presented as coherence.
