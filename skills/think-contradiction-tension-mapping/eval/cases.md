# Eval cases: think-contradiction-tension-mapping

> Trigger and output evaluation, derived from `SKILL.md` and `evidence/dossier.md`. No runner yet (a harness is deferred); these are the cases to check by hand, or to wire into evals later.

## Should trigger

- "Every two years we recentralize to fix the chaos, then decentralize to fix the bottleneck, then recentralize again. I'm sick of the swing - help me map this as a tension to manage instead of a problem to keep re-solving."
- "Our leadership is split: half want hard standardization across teams, half want local autonomy, and each side treats the other as the obstacle. Neither is going to win outright. Give me a both/and map so both camps can own a shared purpose."
- "We keep splitting the difference between moving fast and being careful, and we end up with the downside of both - shipping junk *and* moving slowly. I think these are interdependent, not a choice. Map the upsides and downsides of each and tell me the warning signs we've overcorrected."
- "Stability versus change is a permanent fight here - too much of either kills us. Build the polarity map: greater purpose, deeper fear, the four quadrants, and the early signals that we've leaned too far one way."
- "We can't resolve candor versus diplomacy on this team - we need both, forever. Lay out each pole's upside and downside, the signs we've gone too far toward bluntness or too far toward smoothing things over, and the actions that pull us back."
- "Our growth motion oscillates between all-in self-serve and all-in enterprise sales, and each lurch wrecks the other. I don't want you to pick one - I want a structure to manage the interdependence over time, with action steps for when we've drifted too far."

## Should NOT trigger (wrong tool / near-miss)

- "Speed versus quality is killing us - but I think it's actually a false trade-off. Can we separate the requirements so we get both at once instead of choosing?" (the ask is to *dissolve* the trade-off by separating the conflicting requirements in time / space / scale / condition - that is `think-contradiction-resolution`. Tension mapping is for a tension declared *permanent*; this user believes it can be made to disappear, so resolve it first and only declare a polarity if dissolution fails.)
- "We have three options for our pricing model and need to commit to one this quarter - help me weigh them and choose the best." (a real either/or with a right answer to be selected is `think-decision-option-review`; tension mapping's defining refusal is *not* to choose, and forcing it here would manufacture false balance and stall a decision that should be made.)
- "Give me a full rounded read on this reorg proposal - the facts, the upside, the risks, my gut, the alternatives, and the process - so I'm not missing an angle." (running several separated content lenses over one object for a balanced read is `think-parallel-perspectives-review`; tension mapping is not lenses on one thing, it is a structure for two interdependent poles of a single tension.)
- "Should we restate the actual problem here before we do anything? We keep arguing but I'm not even sure we've framed what we're solving." (the problem is unframed, not a two-sided interdependent tension yet - that is `think-problem-restatement`; reaching for a polarity map on a vague problem invents a tension that may not be there. Frame it first.)
- "Assume our platform bet has already failed two years out - work backward to everything that could have caused it." (imagining one specified failure and reasoning to its causes is `think-premortem`; tension mapping is not failure-anchored and does not map causes of one outcome.)
- "Should we commit accounting fraud or stay honest? Map both sides fairly." (not a polarity - there is a right answer; drawing a both/and map here would dignify a wrong pole. This is the dominant misuse the diagnosis step exists to catch; refuse and say why.)

## Output checks (a good output must)

- [ ] Run the **diagnosis** first and confirm "polarity," not a solvable problem, a dissolvable trade-off, or a real choice - and route away (to `think-contradiction-resolution` or `think-decision-option-review`) if it is not a polarity.
- [ ] Name **two interdependent poles** in neutral, both-positive language - neither pole the villain - and show the interdependence (having all of one with no cost to the other would be a loss).
- [ ] State a **greater purpose** (top, genuinely shared) and a **deeper fear** of getting the downside of both poles (bottom).
- [ ] Fill **all four quadrants** (the upside and downside of each pole), with each pole's downside visibly corrected by the other pole's upside.
- [ ] Give each pole concrete, observable **early-warning signs** of sliding into its downside.
- [ ] Give each pole concrete **action steps**, including how to pull back toward the neglected pole when warnings fire - a management routine, not a static diagram.
- [ ] Deliver the **both/and polarity map** artifact, not prose, and do **not** resolve it into "pick one pole."
- [ ] Not overclaim: keep to a structure for managing a genuine polarity; the evidence is tier C and transferred, and this is not a measured improvement in decisions.

## Value vs unaided baseline

Asked the same question, a strong model tends to do one of two unhelpful things: it quietly picks the pole it finds more defensible and argues for it (collapsing a polarity into a choice), or it produces a flat "on one hand / on the other hand" balance that smooths the tension into a bland compromise - which is exactly the false-consensus failure that gets the downside of both poles. It rarely runs the polarity-or-problem diagnosis first (so it will happily "manage both" on a question that has a right answer), and it rarely produces the operational half that makes the method work: the interlocking upside/downside quadrants where each pole's downside is the other's upside, the shared greater purpose and deeper fear that give two warring camps something to own together, and - most of all - the observable early-warning signs and concrete action steps that turn a static diagram into a management routine. This skill forces that discipline: a genuine interdependence test, neutral both-positive poles, four interlocking quadrants, a shared purpose and fear, per-pole warning signs, and per-pole action steps - plus a hard refusal to run on a solvable problem or to let the map become a permanent excuse for never deciding. It converts a destructive pendulum or a values clash into a shared instrument for oscillating deliberately around the upper half of both poles.
