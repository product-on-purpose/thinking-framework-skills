# Eval cases: think-three-horizons

> Trigger and output evaluation, derived from `SKILL.md` and `evidence/dossier.md`. No runner yet (a harness is deferred); these are the cases to check by hand, or to wire into evals later.

## Should trigger

- "Half the team wants to defend our core business and half wants to chase the disruptive new thing. Map out how the old model declines and the new one grows at the same time, and show me what is in the contested middle."
- "We keep jumping from 'how things work today' straight to 'the future we want' and skipping everything in between. Lay the present, the transition, and the future on one canvas and tell me which of our current bets actually carry the future versus just prop up the old system."
- "Our incumbent system is clearly losing fit and there are faint signs of a different future already inside the business. Build a three-horizons map - name what is failing in the present, the seeds of the future, and classify the transition moves."
- "Some of our 'innovation' initiatives look transformative but I suspect they are really just keeping the legacy machine alive. Read each one as either carrying the new future or being captured by the old system, and locate where our energy is actually going."
- "We need a shared picture for a strategy offsite: the declining present, the emerging future, and the messy transition zone, with where each of us is standing - so the H1 defenders and the H3 visionaries can talk across instead of past each other."
- "Show me the transition from our seat-licensed business to a usage-based, AI-native one as three overlapping curves, and flag where we are at risk of measuring the new-horizon work with old-horizon metrics."

## Should NOT trigger (wrong tool / near-miss)

- "We've already chosen the future we want - fully product-led in three years. Map the milestones and preconditions backward to a step we can take now." (one fixed desired future with a single path derived back to today is `think-backcasting`; Three Horizons holds three curves at once and produces an orientation map, not a route, and adds the managed decline of the present and the two-directional transition read backcasting has no analogue for.)
- "We can't predict how the market will move, so build a handful of contrasting external futures we don't control and tell me which of our moves survive all of them." (a set of divergent, equally-plausible external futures held in parallel with a robustness read is `think-scenario-planning`; Three Horizons asserts one declining present and one desired future and reads the transition between them, and must not be used to launder a single preferred narrative.)
- "We're launching the self-serve free tier - trace the first, second, and third-order consequences of that one move radiating outward." (one consequence map radiating from one change is `think-futures-wheel`, not three time-curves with a contested transition zone.)
- "Churn keeps rising - take me down from the symptom through the patterns and structures to the mental models underneath it." (driving one problem down through layers of causation is `think-iceberg-model`, a vertical depth probe; Three Horizons is a horizontal time canvas.)
- "Just tell me whether to launch the free tier or not, and give me the answer." (Three Horizons produces a shared orientation for dialogue, not a defended decision; it is a sensemaking scaffold, not a recommendation engine - settle expectations or use a decision method instead.)
- "Summarize what the team shipped this quarter for the board update." (unrelated.)

## Output checks (a good output must)

- [ ] Name the system in transition and the actor's vantage point in one line.
- [ ] Set the axis explicitly to **degree of transformation, not calendar time**, and not read the horizons as fixed time bands.
- [ ] Describe **H1** as the dominant-now system AND state what is failing / losing fit in it.
- [ ] Describe **H3** as a qualitatively different desired future AND identify its "pockets of the future in the present" (concrete present-day seeds).
- [ ] Populate the **H2** transition zone with actual moves - the messy middle is not skipped.
- [ ] Classify every H2 move as **H2-plus** (carries H3) or **H2-minus** (captured by H1) - the two-directional read is present.
- [ ] Locate the **actor** on the canvas and name where attention and energy actually flow, including any mismatch.
- [ ] Include the **governance implication** (different horizons need different metrics; H1 governance not misapplied to H2/H3).
- [ ] Frame the map as a **shared orientation for dialogue**, not a forecast or a defended decision.
- [ ] Not overclaim: keep to a sensemaking scaffold that surfaces transition dynamics; the evidence is tier C and transferred, and this is not a foresight or outcome method.

## Value vs unaided baseline

Asked the same question, a strong model tends to flatten the picture into a two-state "current state vs future state" comparison, or to produce a linear roadmap from now to the goal - both of which skip the contested middle and treat the future as a single endpoint. It rarely holds the declining present, the transition zone, and the emerging future as three simultaneous overlapping curves; it almost never performs the signature move of reading the transition zone in two directions (which "innovative" moves genuinely carry the future versus which are captured by the incumbent and merely prop it up); and it does not locate the actor and surface the mismatch between stated intent and where energy actually flows. Crucially, an unaided model will happily treat the horizons as time bands - the single most destructive failure - and will measure new-horizon work with old-horizon metrics without noticing. This skill forces the discipline: an explicit degree-of-transformation axis (not calendar), a named declining present with its failing fit, an emerging future with real present-day seeds, a populated H2 zone with every move classified H2-plus or H2-minus, the actor located with an honest energy read, and a governance note that different horizons need different metrics - delivered as a sensemaking scaffold, not a forecast or a decision.
